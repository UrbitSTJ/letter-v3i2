In this issue of the \emph{Urbit Systems Technical Journal}, we are delighted to present a number of topical pieces, most saliently \patp{dozreg-toplud}'s revisitation of subject knowledge analysis based on his active development and implementation.  SKA promises an era of faster Nock compilation.  \patp{lagrev-nocfep} contributes two pieces, one on how the SKI combinator calculus, long a mainstream staple of fundamental computer science, can be rigorously related to the Nock combinator calculus; and the other on the Urbit numerical stack, which first began development in 2020 and continues to break new ground in deterministic numerical computing.  Longtime former Tlon CTO \patp{wicdev-wisryt}, a chief architect of many parts of Urbit itself, explains Hoon's input/output model in a piece from the archives.  As always, each article selected culminates long and careful design and development work, and we are proud to present them to the Urbit community.

Urbit programming has continued to be revolutionized, both by new programming models and agent-driven development.  We excitedly anticipate new waves of both userspace and kernel-level products in the coming months and years.

As of 2025, USTJ has become a sponsored project of the Groundwire Foundation, a new nonprofit entity dedicated to Urbit's core development and improving support for Bitcoin integrations into the Urbit ecosystem.  We are grateful for their patronage and the educational and documentary mandate.

Computer science happens in the trenches.  \tombstone{}
